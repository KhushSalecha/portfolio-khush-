FestOS — College Fest Architecture

College fest platform architecture and database planning for 2,400+ attendees.

Category
System Architecture · UI/UX Design · Requirements Engineering · Data Modeling
Timeline
2026 · Planning & Specification Phase
Target Volume
2,400+ Student Attendees · 150+ Core Organizing Team Members
Key Disciplines
Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), Database Schema Design, Wireframing, Shift Scheduling

Spearheaded the technical requirements analysis and system architecture for a college fest digital portal, modeling database capacity for 2,400+ students and bifurcating administration workflows from public attendee experiences.

Project Context & Objective

University fests are logistical marathons involving multi-track cultural and technical competitions, merchandise distributions, attendee ticketing, and real-time scheduling. Without a unified system, information fragments across spreadsheets, WhatsApp channels, and paper logs, resulting in entry bottlenecks and miscommunication.

FestOS was initiated to establish a robust digital infrastructure blueprint capable of smoothly orchestrating operations for an anticipated student audience of 2,400+ participants and a multi-tiered student organizing committee.

Dual-Portal Architecture & Role-Based Access

A fundamental finding of the requirements analysis was the need to partition the application into two dedicated portals with distinct operational scopes:

  • Student Attendee Portal: Focused on friction-free event exploration, digital pass generation, workshop registrations, schedule notifications, and live competition result boards.
  • Core Committee Command Portal: Restricted administrative hub managing volunteer shift rosters, volunteer roles and contact trees, stage equipment logistics, entry gate QR ticket scanning, and merchandise dispatch tracking.

Database Schema & Scale Planning

With 2,400 attendees actively interacting during high-concurrency event registration bursts, the database architecture required thoughtful relational modeling:

  • Normalized Entity Structures: Explicit tables for Students, Registrations, Events, Volunteers, Shifts, and MerchandiseLogs.
  • Concurrency Control: Strategies to prevent race conditions during capped-capacity workshop bookings (using row-level locking and atomic reservation checks).
  • Audit Logging: Granular tracking of ticket validation timestamps and check-in checkpoints to maintain crowd safety audits.

UI/UX Planning & Wireframing

The project synthesized team feedback into structured UI wireframes prioritizing mobile responsiveness—recognizing that 95% of students navigate fest platforms on smartphones amidst loud, crowded festival venues. Key design elements included high-contrast typography, offline-cached digital passes, and instant search filtering across 30+ simultaneous events.

Key Engineering Takeaways

This project deepened my appreciation for the foundational pre-coding phases of software development: meticulous requirements gathering, identifying edge cases before writing a line of code, and translating human logistical challenges into structured database schemas.
